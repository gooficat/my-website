# 3D Printing Deals

If you don't have a 3D printer and don't have a lot to spend, you actually have quite a few options.
The first is to go on facebook marketplace, vinted, ebay, and gumtree, and other websites in your local area, and start looking. I found my 2 "proper" printers cheap on Vinted and Facebook Marketplace.
Be careful with facebook marketplace. It can be dangerous. Prefer if they can post it to you.

If you want a 3D printer to tinker with, look for an Ender printer. An Ender 3 v1 is usually a good pick. Don't spend more than £40 on one. An Ender 5 is another, usually a bit more expensive pick.

If you aren't looking to tinker but are willing to do repairs, it's common to find a Neptune 3/4, Anycubic Kobra 2/3/X, or an Adventurer 5 M/X. A rarer find is a Bambu printer, but they're very good if they work.

In some places you really can't find these used printers. You could look into a full on self-build. I've seen people in India source aluminium extrusions (a specially shaped stick of metal that you can slot T-nuts into, and run V-wheels on) for very cheap, and build large format printers for as little as $200.

The most common pick ends up being an ender though, and that's what I did. So here's some deals related to Ender mods. These may be good for a self-build too.
If you want to self-build, you can look into existing designs. A bed-slinger, similar to an Ender, is usually an easy first option. Ender 3-style printers can be eventually rebuilt into box-frame CoreXY printers too.

I also own an EasyThreed X1 printer. these are tiny, toy printers that are actually made from mostly standard parts. If all else fails, you might find one of these. Avoid the K-series ones at all costs. You want something with proper endstops, and that runs on metal rods. You can buy them a proper extruder.

I recommend learning printer terminology, and what the parts do. Most people nowadays haven't a clue even if they own a Bambu printer.
